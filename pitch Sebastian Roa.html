Ship plan

How I'd ship Cortex

The autonomy dial per segment, where it sits on the trust ladder, how it deploys, what it returns, and how I keep it safe.

01 · Dial

Autonomy per segment

Experienced PMs use supervised autonomy: after approving context and tone, Cortex prepares and validates an update and story proposal for PM review. PMs new to Cortex use assisted autonomy, with an additional planned review of evidence and scope before drafting. Both segments retain human approval of final outputs, external communications, and commitments. The additional assisted checkpoint is not yet implemented.
02 · Ladder

Rung + eval gate

Cortex currently operates as a supervised local prototype: it prepares and validates updates and story proposals, while the PM reviews claims, scope, and both outputs before external action. Advancement toward bounded-autonomous preparation requires six weeks with at least 30 supervised runs, at least 95% pass on each quality eval (EV-1–EV-4), 100% pass on evaluated safety cases (EV-5), and zero unauthorized actions or confidential disclosures. Missing evidence prevents advancement.
A planned incident log will track errors, near-misses, refusals, and bound stops. Unexpected issues require review and closure, and any unauthorized action or disclosure resets the six-week window. This gate has not yet been met, and production incident tracking remains unimplemented. Publication and commitments remain human-owned.
03 · Deploy

Where it runs & who owns it

Cortex’s planned deployment is an always-on service supporting scheduled and on-demand jobs with approved context and tone. Each run allows up to five drafts, four revisions, ten minutes, a 60-second model request timeout, a $0.10 estimated spending threshold, and ten proposed stories. Runs for the same project must not overlap. Human review, publication, and commitments remain PM-owned.

I will review outputs and handle escalations, with an engineering backup for technical failures and a security/privacy contact for suspected disclosures. Those contacts and routing still need setup. Rollback will pause triggers, stop jobs, restore an approved version, and validate recovery before resuming. Stopping local execution may not cancel an in-flight request.
Monitoring will track eval results, escalations, incidents, costs, timing, revisions, and overlapping-run attempts. The service, dashboards, alerts, and service-wide spending controls remain planned. The current prototype saves outputs locally and sends no notifications.
04 · ROI

Value, not activity

- Outcome: At least 90% of completed proposals have both the update and stories approved with only minor presentation edits during the six-week pilot. Report completion and escalation rates separately.

- Cost-to-serve: At least 30% lower total cost per approved task than an equivalent manual baseline. Include actual model/service costs, PM preparation, review and rework time, and failed attempts. Establish the baseline and labor rate before claiming savings.

- Trust incidents: Zero unauthorized actions or confidential disclosures across all attempted runs. Review and close unexpected incidents; any violation resets the six-week clean window.

These are planned targets, not achieved results, and remain separate from the autonomy advancement gate.
05 · Governance

How I keep it safe

- Compliance: Use only necessary, approved project data, restrict confidential information, and keep secrets and unnecessary personal identifiers out of prompts and traces. Apply the agreed memory policies and approve artifact retention before production. Filtering, redaction, expiry, and deletion controls remain partly unimplemented.

- Safety: Keep human approval of context, tone, claims, scope, and final outputs. Publication, commitments, ticket changes, and merges remain human-owned. Cortex refuses known explicit rule overrides, but broader injection protection and service-wide pause/stop controls remain incomplete.

- Reliability: Limit runs to five drafts, four revisions, ten minutes, a 60-second API timeout, a $0.10 estimated spending threshold, and ten stories. Retry missing or invalid data up to three times; stop on connection failures and hold incomplete outputs for review. Production alerts and service-wide spending controls still need implementation.

- Strategy: Widen preparation autonomy for experienced PMs only after six weeks and at least 30 supervised runs meet the 95% quality and 100% evaluated safety thresholds, with zero unauthorized actions or disclosures, closed unexpected issues, and PM-owner approval. New PMs remain assisted. Any advancement requires an implemented and evaluated workflow change; the gate has not yet been met.
Build insights

What I learned

The honest three: where it hurt, what clicked, and the moment something changed.

😵 Friction

The biggest friction was getting the draft and critic to agree on what the evidence supported. Cortex sometimes added unsupported wording, while the critic sometimes rejected valid claims. Fixing this required clearer source attribution—using the PRD to establish scope and issue #825 to support the analytics-review work—and checking that the critic still rejected the fake 80% metric.

🧠 Learning

I learned that claims need evidence supporting their exact meaning: the PRD can establish scope, while an issue supports specific planned work. I also learned that a critic can make mistakes, so I need to test both valid claims and deliberately faulty inputs. Finally, shipping an agent requires clear stopping rules, saved traces, and human approval alongside useful outputs.

💡 Aha

My aha moment was realizing that autonomy should be earned through evidence. A successful demo is a starting point; Cortex needs consistent quality and safety results over the six-week supervised window before I widen its autonomy. This changed how I would design my next agent: define the human boundaries and advancement gate from the beginning.
